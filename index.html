Time to meet your maker.

It's me, hi. I'm the maker - it's me.

Hi, I’m Annabelle and I’m happy you’re here! I am SO many things and it seems nearly impossible to describe all of that here in a little baby beginner website. I am a whimsical, self-reflective, joy-seeking girly who loves people. I'm continually learning to embrace the messiness of being a human and building a life that is aligned with my authentic self. I love to play, spend time with my people, sit in the sun, watch football, travel, and read books (with my eyes and ears)

Disclaimer: This list is incredibly limited and in no way represents all of who I am and the things I enjoy.

A huge part of who I am and what I enjoy is creating. It’s almost everywhere for me. I see potential projects no matter where I am. It all starts with a spark - I see something I like or feel drawn toward and immediately my brain is popping off with ideas of how I could make it or include some detail of it into something else. I’m rarely trying to make an exact copy (Halloween costume characters excluded). I’m excited by things I would enjoy having, but also by things that I know would be fun or meaningful to people I care about.
Once I’ve got the idea and a commitment behind it, I’m fully locked in. I start to look up materials and ways to DIY the details and then narrow down into what will be best in the end. Honestly most of the time I’m not choosing the solutions that are the fastest, easiest, or matching my current skill level. My main goal is always to choose what’s the highest quality end result. This is about the point when my delusion sets in and I ignore the realities of whether I currently possess that skill or not. I mostly operate on the assumption that if I like it and I want it done then I’ll figure out a way to make it happen - and I do, but the middle is MESSY.
As you could imagine, jumping in assuming I can do anything regardless of how much prior knowledge a person would actually need for that thing is…wild. Very often I encounter moments where things are going wrong and (surprise) I don’t know what I’m doing. I can keep trying at it for a bit before it turns into a slew of self-talk I wouldn’t want people to know I’m saying. I get frustrated and just don’t know why I always seem to get myself into this kind of situation and often get to a point where I quit - except I really don’t actually quit because my brain is still working things out in the background despite my best efforts of being negative about it.

After rage quitting and being grumpy about it, I come back to the project. It might be a few minutes, hours or days, but I always come back. Sometimes I try something again, try something new or go a totally different direction. I know what I want and I know I can make it happen, I just need to be a little dramatic about it first. That's the messy middle, the cycle I go through multiple times per project, or only once if I'm lucky. Go in with confidence, make a mistake, get grumpy, quit(ish).

Rinse and repeat.
Okay, so maybe this is sounding like it wouldn’t be fun. If you talk to me in the middle of that cycle, I’d tell you it’s not and I don’t know why I choose to do that to myself! But in the moments of success and the glimmers of that finished product, it really is so incredibly satisfying. And the way it feels to complete something, look at it and know I just did that? THE BEST. I want to share it, share my process, the hows and whys of what I did along the way, the reason for the details, everything. When it’s for someone else, I get to watch them notice the details, light up at something I put in there just for them, and feel seen. That’s definitely one of my favorite parts.
I often get compliments on the things I’ve created and that feels nice. Of course I am glad to know that other people like and appreciate what I’ve done. I’m proud of the things I put out into the world. But the truth of it is that, while I do create for other people - to show them love, bring joy or be appreciated - the whole process is for me. The number of details I add may or may not be seen or understood or appreciated by other people, but that doesn’t matter as much as knowing that I made it happen.
And in the end, it’s not just about what I made. The end results are good (like, really good) but it’s so much more than that. The finished product is a physical representation of every choice and minute spent struggling and being confused and quitting and coming back and eventually succeeding.

Time will pass and I'll forget how much stress and pressure I felt and move on to my next idea and start the whole thing over again. I'll be doing this the rest of my life in some way or another because It's at the very core of who I am.

I always do good shit.
